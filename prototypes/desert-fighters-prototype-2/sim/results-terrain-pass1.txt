── VERDICT ──
Terrain did NOT reach the worm's numbers. Pass 1 gets close on the headline count (525 vs 579 strong triples, 0 orphans)
but misses on everything else: the weakest card owns 9 combos (worm 18), only 10/35 cards sit within ±5 ΔWin (worm 18),
the card spread is almost twice as wide (SD 12.3 vs 7.0), and choosing the order is worth less (+19 points vs +27).

Where the variety came from: the tile-aware ABILITIES, not the tiles. Random tiles on their own cost variety: the same
cards on a plain Dune lane make 565 strong triples, random terrain drops that to 356 and the weakest card to 3 combos.
Pass 1 won back 169 triples, and the six changed cards account for most of it (41 → 244 owned combos between them).
Nearly all of that is one engine: Claw Strike turns the front into Quicksand, the front cards rotate every clash, and the
🧭 cards (Hyena, Hex Scales) fire on every move.

Behemoth is the real problem the worm was solving. Without a tax on a 12/10, Behemoth owns 191-194 combos and most of the
top 40 triples are "two cheap enablers + Behemoth" (Curse Bite / Deathfeather / Healer Doe + Behemoth). Terrain is shared
by both sides and never touches stats, so it can't push back on raw size. A fair comparison with the worm would need
Behemoth re-costed first; I didn't do that, since it's outside this task.

Tiles that did little:
  * Mirage: Rock/Mirage make only 0.11-0.15 abilities fizzle per fight. Most abilities pick "the first / a random
    enemy" from the cards they CAN target, so Mirage quietly redirects them instead of blocking anything you can see.
  * Quicksand in the middle or back column: only front cards clash, so it does nothing there. Quicksand is a front-only tile.
  * Swap tiles (Falcon): 9 combos. Swapping front and back only matters when the two tiles differ in a way the
    lineup cares about, which is rare with random tiles.
  * Oasis, Rock and front Quicksand all fire about once per fight. They do act; they just don't create combos by themselves.

Complexity without depth:
  * Mirage is Rock minus the silence. Two tiles share one rule ("can't be targeted"), and the difference is hard to read.
  * Shared tiles are symmetric. Whatever a tile does for your front card it also does for theirs, so most tiles shift
    both sides equally and the only lever is ordering. The order value went DOWN (27 → 19-20).
  * "First enemy" now means "first enemy an ability can reach". Under Mirage an ability silently skips to the second
    card. It is correct by the rules but surprising to watch, and the preview has to re-derive it.
  * The 🧭 trigger needs slot tracking, an entry-resolution loop with a pass cap (two movers can shove each other forever),
    and quicksand rules about who sinks once. That's a lot of engine for one trigger, though it is the trigger that made
    the combos.
  * Oasis doubles death and hurt triggers as well, so a few cards get very swingy on one tile. It's readable in the log
    but hard to predict from the prep screen.
