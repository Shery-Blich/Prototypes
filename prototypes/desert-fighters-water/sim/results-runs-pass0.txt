What it says
1. Mana's variety gain is from the per-card COSTS: the flat-cost control plays exactly like water off (same 21 lineups,
   Hyena 51%). Cheap cards (Jackal, cost 1) take over the fielding.
2. Uses' variety gain is mostly from ANY per-card cap: flat 3 uses gets most of it (51 vs 61 greedy lineups, 115 vs 114
   for the planner). Strong cards with few uses force rotation, but so does giving everyone 3.
3. Neither creates decisions yet. Every policy except random wins every fight it takes and extracts 100% of the time.
   With the whole squad (9-13 cards) to pick from, the designed encounters (tuned for 3 random cards) are trivial, so
   the best move is always "the cheapest sure win". The planner's edge (+24) in off/uses is pathing (it finds the
   6-battle route; greedy takes the easy-first 5-battle route), not water. In mana the edge shrinks to +4.5.
   Uses never binds: runs end with ~30 uses unspent.

Known metric limits
- "close calls" (options within 10 score of the best) reads ~100% everywhere: when every option wins, every option is
  close. It only means something once fights can be lost.
- Greedy's path rule (easiest battle first) confounds value of planning; a "most battles" greedy path would isolate
  the water decisions.
