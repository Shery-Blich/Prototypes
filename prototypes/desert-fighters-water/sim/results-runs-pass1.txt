What it says
1. Uses with per-card water is the only setup that clearly creates decisions: traps triple vs water off (21% vs 7%)
   and double vs the flat control (10%). The decision is "when do I spend my strong card's single use".
2. Uses rotation (variety) comes from ANY cap: flat 2 uses gets as much variety as per-card uses (13.4 effective
   cards, 200 lineups). Per-card uses adds the decisions on top, and pushes recruits into play (Trample Share,
   Hex Scales show up in the most-fielded list).
3. Mana's variety comes from the per-card COSTS (flat costs play like water off again, as in pass 0), but its
   decisions barely beat the controls (traps 11% vs 9% flat, 7% off). The planner's overrides are mostly "chump"
   plays: it sends a cheap card into a fight it expects to lose to save water (it gives up 72 win-rate points when it
   overrides). Mana also costs the most score (133 vs 169 off).
4. Value of planning is +10 to +15 everywhere and the ±5 noise swallows the differences: 100 runs × 8 rollouts is
   not enough to separate modes on that number. Traps and overrides separate them; value of planning does not yet.

Verdict for pass 1: start with USES. It is the only mode whose per-card water beats both controls on decisions,
and it keeps variety high without the score hit mana takes.

Caveats
- Run conditions (all-hard encounters, 6-card squad) are sim knobs. The game's encounters would need retuning for
  whichever mode ships; as designed they are too easy once you pick from a squad.
- Water values are the pass-0 formula from solo ΔWin, untuned. Uses −1 makes 8 cards single-use.
- Traps are measured with 8 rollouts; water off's 7% is the noise floor.
