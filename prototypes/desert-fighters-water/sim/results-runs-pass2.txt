What it says
1. Pass 1 holds with 3× the runs and 2× the rollouts: m1 doubles the traps of flat 2 (20% vs 11%) and value of
   planning is now clearly above water off (+19 vs +9, the gap is ~2.5× the noise). Pass 1's +15±6 was noise-limited.
2. It is not a smooth "more spread = more decisions" curve, it is a step. Shallow (2 single-use cards) is no better
   than flat. m1 (11) and steep (16) are the same. What creates the decisions is MANY STRONG CARDS WITH EXACTLY ONE
   USE: "when do I spend my one-shot Warthog / Buffalo / Hyena" is the choice the obvious play gets wrong.
   Giving weak cards more uses (steep) adds nothing.
3. Variety comes from any cap (flat 2 already 13.5 effective cards, the most winning lineups). Per-card uses adds the
   decisions on top without losing that variety (14.6 effective cards for m1).
4. The price: m1/steep are harder. Planner score drops ~16-20 vs off/shallow, greedy ~25-30. A player who doesn't
   plan pays more for it, which is the point, but the encounters would need tuning for it.

Recommendation: keep USES with the m1 shape (strong cards single-use, the rest 2-3). Mana is not pursued
(pass 1: its decisions sat near its controls, and its planner "chump-plays" cheap cards to save water).
